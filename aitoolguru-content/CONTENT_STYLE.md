## 1. Tone and Voice

### Who We Are

AI Tool Guru is the straightforward friend who knows tech inside out and tells you the truth, even when it is uncomfortable. We are not cheerleaders for every product that launches. We are the person you call before buying software, because we will save you from expensive mistakes.

### Voice Principles

**Clear, not clever.** Every sentence should earn its place. If a reader has to read something twice, rewrite it.

**Authoritative, not arrogant.** We know our subject, but we do not talk down. A reader who has never used AI should feel welcomed, not patronised.

**Honest, not promotional.** If a tool has flaws, we say so. If a cheaper alternative is better for most users, we recommend it. Affiliate commissions do not override integrity.

**UK-focused, not US-centric.** Pricing in pounds sterling. Spelling in British English. Examples from British businesses. References to UK regulations and contexts.

**Specific, not vague.** "A freelance graphic designer in Manchester uses it to..." beats "This tool is great for creative professionals."

### What We Avoid

- Em dashes (—) and double hyphens (--). Use commas, colons, or separate sentences instead.
- Jargon without explanation. If we must use technical terms, define them in plain English.
- Hyperbole. "Game-changing" and "revolutionary" are banned unless we can prove it.
- Generic praise. "Easy to use" tells the reader nothing. "New staff learn it in 10 minutes without training" does.
- Passive voice. "The tool was praised by users" becomes "Users praised the tool."

Every tool review follows this structure. Deviation requires editorial approval.

### Article Header

```

_Quinn, Content Director, AI Tool Guru — Last updated [Month Year]_
```

### The Bottom Line

A 2-3 paragraph executive summary that answers: what is this article about, who is it for, and what will they learn? Include a quick reference table of top picks by category.

### Tool Sections (repeat for each tool)

```
## [Number]. [Tool Name]

**Best for:** [One-line category positioning]

**Star rating:** [X.Y / 5]

**Starting price:** [Price]

### Overview
[2-3 paragraphs. What the tool does, who uses it, and why it matters. Include a specific example of a UK business or professional using it.]

### Key Features
[5-7 bullet points. Each feature gets a bold title and 1-2 sentences explaining what it does and why it matters. Include a specific use case where possible.]

### Pros
[4-5 bullet points. Genuine advantages backed by evidence or specific examples.]

### Cons
[3-4 bullet points. Honest drawbacks. Every tool has them. If you cannot find cons, you have not researched enough.]

[1-2 paragraphs summarising sentiment from Reddit, G2, Trustpilot, Capterra, or CQC reports. Quote specific reviews where helpful. Include negative sentiment too.]

[**Editor's Choice** or **Also Great** with a one-paragraph recommendation. Be specific about who should buy it and who should look elsewhere.]

[Affiliate link with clear disclosure.]
```

A closing section that provides stack recommendations for different scenarios (startup, growing business, enterprise) with total costs.

## 3. How to Write Engaging Tool Descriptions

### The Specific Example Rule

Every tool overview must include at least one specific example of a real person using it in a real situation. Not "A marketer uses this to..." but "A freelance copywriter in Edinburgh uses it to draft client emails in the morning and generate social captions at lunch."

### The Pain-Relief Framework

Structure descriptions around problems solved, not features listed:

1. **The problem:** What was broken before this tool?
2. **The solution:** How does this tool fix it?
3. **The result:** What changed for the user?

Example: "A care provider in Leicester used paper MAR charts that went missing, got wet, or became illegible. Birdie digitised the process. Now carers record medications in real time with photo evidence, and managers receive instant alerts for missed doses."

### Feature Writing

Each feature should answer three questions:
- What does it do? (The functionality)
- Why does it matter? (The benefit)
- Who uses it? (The specific user)

Bad: "AI-powered scheduling optimises rotas."
Good: "AI-powered scheduling generates optimised rotas that balance staff preferences, skill requirements, and labour cost targets. A care provider in Sheffield reduced overtime costs by 18% after implementation."

### Active Voice Check

Before publishing, scan for these passive voice patterns and rewrite:
- "was used by" -> "used"
- "is considered" -> "consider"
- "has been praised" -> "users praised"
- "can be integrated" -> "integrates with"

## 4. How to Extract and Present User Sentiment

### Sources to Monitor

| Platform | Best For | How to Search |
|---|---|---|
| Reddit (r/smallbusinessuk, r/recruitment, r/SEO, r/freelancewriters) | Honest complaints, real-world usage | Search tool name + "review" or "vs" |
| G2 | Enterprise and B2B sentiment | Filter by company size and industry |
| Trustpilot | Consumer and small business reviews | Sort by recent, read 3-5 star reviews carefully |
| Capterra | Software comparisons and feature feedback | Check "pros and cons" sections |
| Glassdoor | Employee perspective on company stability | Useful for predicting tool longevity |
| CQC reports | Healthcare-specific compliance evidence | Search for tool name in inspection reports |

### How to Synthesise Reviews

1. Read 20-30 reviews across multiple platforms
2. Identify recurring themes (both positive and negative)
3. Note specific examples and quotes that illustrate each theme
4. Count rough sentiment ratios (e.g., "80% of G2 users praise X; the main complaint is Y")
5. Write a paragraph that captures the overall sentiment honestly

### The Balance Rule

Every "What Users Say" section must include:
- At least one specific positive example with a quote or paraphrase
- At least one specific criticism or limitation
- Attribution to the platform where the sentiment was found

Never present only glowing reviews. Readers distrust perfection.

### Example Format

```
G2 reviewers rate [Tool] 4.5/5, calling it "[specific praise]." A Trustpilot reviewer from [location] said [specific benefit]. However, several Reddit users on r/[subreddit] complained that [specific drawback]. The main complaint across review sites: [recurring issue].
```

## 5. Affiliate Disclosure Rules

### Legal Requirement

All content must comply with the UK's Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 and the ASA CAP Code. Failure to disclose affiliate relationships can result in enforcement action.

### Disclosure Placement

1. **In-article:** Include the standard disclosure in the footer of every article
2. **Per-link:** Individual affiliate links should be marked "(affiliate link)"
3. **Homepage:** A sitewide disclosure in the footer: "AI Tool Guru uses affiliate links. We earn a commission when you purchase through our links, at no extra cost to you."

### Standard Disclosure Text

```
Some links are affiliate links: we earn a commission at no extra cost to you. We only recommend tools we have tested and believe in.
```

### What We Do NOT Do

- Write positive reviews because of affiliate relationships
- Rank tools higher based on commission rates
- Recommend tools we have not researched thoroughly
- Hide affiliate relationships in link shorteners or redirects
- Accept payment for placement in "best of" lists

### Editorial Independence

Affiliate relationships are disclosed to readers but do not influence editorial decisions. A tool with no affiliate programme receives the same honest review as one that pays 30% commission. If a recommended tool has no affiliate programme, we still recommend it and earn nothing.

## 6. Formatting and Technical Standards

### Markdown Conventions

- Use ATX-style headers (`# ## ###`)
- Tables for pricing and quick reference only
- Bullet lists for features, pros, and cons
- Bold for tool names, plan names, and key terms on first mention
- Italics for attribution and meta-information

### British English

| American | British |
|---|---|
| optimize | optimise |
| organize | organise |
| color | colour |
| center | centre |
| program (software) | programme (TV/show); program (computer) |
| license (noun) | licence (noun); license (verb) |
| check | cheque (payment); check (examine) |

### Numbers and Currency

- Prices in GBP with "Approx" for converted prices: "Approx £16/month"
- Use numerals for all numbers, including 1-9: "5 users" not "five users"
- Percentages as numerals with % symbol: "40% increase"

### Punctuation

- No em dashes. Use colons, semicolons, or separate sentences
- Oxford commas: use them ("A, B, and C")
- Quotation marks: double for primary, single for nested
- Full stops after abbreviations: "Dr." "Mr." "etc."

## 7. Review Process

### Before Publishing

1. **Fact-check:** Verify all pricing, features, and claims against the vendor's website
2. **Link-check:** Ensure all affiliate links are tagged correctly
3. **Voice-check:** Read the article aloud. If it sounds stiff, rewrite it
4. **Bias-check:** Ensure cons sections are as detailed as pros sections
5. **Date-check:** Update the "Last updated" date and ensure seasonal references are current

### Update Schedule

- **Quarterly:** Review pricing and feature lists for all active articles
- **Bi-annually:** Refresh user sentiment sections with new review data
- **Annually:** Full content audit. Remove outdated tools, add emerging leaders
